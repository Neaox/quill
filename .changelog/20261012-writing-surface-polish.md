Added: A floating toolbar over selected text, a link, a callout or a table, and a context menu on right-click, both built from one registry of actions so they offer the same things.
Added: Callouts have an Important variant, and their variant can be changed from the toolbar or the menu without retyping.
Added: Table of contents and child documents blocks, with the heading levels and the depth of descendants as options, in the editor, on the reading surface and in exports.
Added: "On this page" follows the reader as they scroll, marking the section in view.
Changed: Editing a table is a compact toolbar and handles on the table. A caret in a cell offers alignment, a settings menu and removal; picking a row or column by its grip offers where to put another, where to move it and whether it stays. A "+" appears on every border, and a guide across the table shows where the line will go.
Fixed: The slash menu scrolls to its last item, and keeps its rows aligned however tall the screen is.
Added: A handle on a table's top-left corner selects the whole table in one press, so it can be copied, cut or cleared; "Select table" is in the context menu too.
Changed: Tables being written are set as the page sets them: column headers and footer rows, header columns, numbers flush right, and identifiers kept on one line. Callouts no longer have a second outline in the editor.
Changed: Menus label their items in a heavier weight than the line of explanation beneath, which is smaller and muted; shortcuts are drawn as key caps.
Fixed: The block menu closes when you click outside it.
Fixed: Dragging a table row or column up by a few pixels moved it a whole line; it now moves only when its middle crosses into the next one.
Added: The context menu works with cells selected: cut, copy and paste, copy as Markdown, and clearing the cells. A copy of cells is an HTML table with tab-separated text, so it pastes into a spreadsheet or a text editor as a table.
Changed: A table's context menu offers only what applies to what is selected: a row's tools for rows, a column's for columns, neither for the whole table, with rows and columns named in the plural, and moves offered only for one.
Fixed: Right-clicking a table's corner or a row or column grip opens the menu on that selection instead of deselecting it, and the gutter's "+" no longer sits over a table's corner handle.
Added: A callout can have a title of its own: the toolbar's Title button (or the context menu) adds one, you type into it where the type's name was, Enter goes on to the body, and Backspace on an empty one, or leaving it empty, removes it. It is written as the callout's `[Title]`.
Added: Click a callout's type name, or the icon beside its title, to change its type from the callout itself.
Changed: Menus of choices open on the choice already made.
Changed: GitHub alerts (`> [!NOTE]`) are callouts in the editor, with a callout's type menu, title (which writes it as the directive, as an alert has no title) and removal, and are written back as the alert they were.
Changed: The toolbar over selected text is laid out as other editors lay one out: block style, then bold, italic, strikethrough and code, then the link, then bulleted, numbered and task lists and the quote as switches that turn on and off, then comment, then a "more" menu with Clear formatting and Put in a callout. The lists' keys switch them off as well as on.
Fixed: The words being linked stay drawn as selected while the link box has the keyboard.
Fixed: Double-clicking a word selects the word and not the space after it, which Windows adds.
Added: Obsidian callouts: a quote opening `> [!tip] Title` is a callout with that title, of any kind Obsidian has (`faq`, `bug`, `success`, …), in the editor and on the reading surface, and is written back as typed. See `docs/product/markdown.md` for everything Quill reads.
Changed: Callouts made in the editor are written as the standard quote (`> [!TIP]`, and `> [!TIP] Title` with a title), which GitHub and Obsidian read, rather than as Quill's own `:::callout`, which is kept for documents written with it.
Added: **Done** in the editor saves what you typed and returns to the page; **Preview** shows your unpublished changes as readers will see them, and a notice on the page says when there are some.
Added: Publish says when there is nothing to publish, and pointing at it (or at Preview) lists what is waiting: how many blocks were added, changed or removed, the words, and where.
Removed: The "Read it" button on the published toast; publishing already takes you to the page.
Added: **Review changes** opens every change since the page was published, with the published words beside yours and the words that differ marked on each side. Any change, or several, can be put back in the draft; it is one step that Undo reverses.
Added: The draft remembers who wrote each block that is waiting to be published. The review and the panels on Publish and Preview say who made each change and when, and Publish asks first, naming who, when some of what would go out was written by someone else.
Changed: In a comparison, a word that was replaced by one sharing its beginning or end has the characters that differ picked out and set bolder (`color`/`colour`, `1.2.3`/`1.2.4`), so a single changed character can be seen. The review has Previous and Next, arrow-key movement between changes, Select all, and says why a change cannot be put back.
Fixed: Putting a change back works on drafts whose blocks carry links, pictures or the editor's blank lines, which it refused before.
Changed: In the review, a line that changed has a small mark in the gutter beside it (`+`, `−`, `≠`, `↕`), outside the text so it cannot be taken for part of the document, and a place where a character was taken out or put in is drawn as a tick on the side that no longer has it.
Added: A navigation tree that opens a level at a time: `GET /api/workspaces/:id/tree/children` returns one page of one parent's children, in a stable order (oldest first, so a new document goes last), each with how many it has beneath it. The sidebar does not use it yet; see ADR-049.
Fixed: The `#` before a heading is not drawn anywhere in the application (the writing surface, the reading page and presentation), where it read as Markdown that had lost a character; the public site keeps it.
Changed: Every date and time in the app is written by the platform in the reader's own language (`Intl`). "Saved 2 hours ago", "Updated 3 days ago" and the like are `<time>` elements that say how long ago and show the whole date and time when hovered, and stay true as time passes: one timer serves every one on the page, set for the earliest moment any of them changes, so a thousand of them cost one wakeup a minute and re-draw only the ones that changed.
Changed: A collection's heading in the left navigation stays at the top of the list while its documents scroll beneath it, and the list scrolls to the document being read.
Changed: Presenter notes are never drawn in the window the room sees. **Presenter view** (the bar, or `P`, or `N`) opens a second window with the section being presented, the next one, the notes, a clock and the controls, and the two windows follow each other over a `BroadcastChannel`; the bar says whether you are presenting from a **Single window** or with the **Presenter view**. See ADR-050.
Added: Typing text between single backticks makes it inline code as the closing backtick is typed (Backspace takes it back), and ArrowRight at the end of inline code steps out of it. Inline code is drawn as a chip with a hairline, in full ink, and wraps cleanly.
Changed: When you press Resume editing or Take over after a lock is lost, a toast says whether it worked and why not; the two buttons say what each does (resuming works if nobody else has taken the document; taking over, for managers, ends their session).
Changed: The presenter window fits the window it is in, whatever its size: the notes, the buttons to step, where you are and the time always, with the section being shown, then the next one, as pictures when there is room and as words when there is not. The scroll wheel (or a trackpad swipe) steps through a presentation, unless the pointer is over something that still has room to scroll. A held key on a remote steps once, not through the deck.
Added: A black screen for presenting: `B`, or the `.` most clickers send, blacks out the room's screen; Escape or the next key brings it back (and the next key goes on). The presenter window has a Black screen button, and either window can blank the room.
Changed: A presenter notes block in the editor says what it is: a small projector-screen icon and "Presenter notes", drawn as a callout draws its title, in the box it already had. It is the editor's label, not part of the document.
Fixed: The arrow keys can leave a callout, presenter notes, details or template block that is the last (or first) thing in a document: the key that would go out of it adds an empty line beside it and moves there, so a keyboard user is no longer shut in.
Added: Using the keyboard in the editor, the cell of a table, or the callout, notes or details block, that the caret is in is outlined in the focus ring's colour, so you can see where you are. Only the keys that move the caret (arrows, Tab, Home, End, and Enter in a cell) draw it: a click, and typing after one, do not, as with focus on a button.
Fixed: In Firefox the caret vanished when you pressed Down or Right from the paragraph above a table (and Right then seemed to do nothing). Down and Right from above a table, and Up and Left from below it, now go into the table's nearest cell in every browser.
Added: Alt+Shift+Arrow (Option+Shift+Arrow on a Mac) moves the caret to the neighbouring table cell in that direction, at its start, without stepping through the text first.
Added: Typing a colon in the editor, at the start of a line or after a space, suggests emoji, narrowing as you type; a chosen or typed emoji is drawn at once: your organisation's own first, then GitHub's. Enter or Tab writes the shortcode, and the `/emoji` picker offers the same list.
Changed: A custom emoji has alt text, which screen readers say in place of the picture: add it when you add the emoji (Settings, Labels and emoji) or edit it beside a saved one. An emoji without one is read as its name in words, not as its shortcode.
Changed: Backspace right after a drawn emoji removes the whole emoji, in every browser. Holding Backspace (or Delete) stops at the start (or end) of a block rather than going on into the block beside it; a separate press joins as before.
Added: Select all goes outwards a step at a time: the block you are in, then what is around it (a list, a callout, a table), then the document. Ctrl+D selects the word and then each next occurrence. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the block (or list item) you are in. Ctrl+/ lists every keyboard shortcut. The emoji menu offers the emoji you chose last first.
Added: Find and replace in the editor, as in VS Code. Ctrl+F opens a find bar with match case, whole word and regular expression toggles, a count ("3 of 12"), Enter and Shift+Enter to step, and every match highlighted; Ctrl+H adds a replace row with replace, replace all (one undo), preserve case and capture groups in a regular expression's replacement; Alt+L searches only the selection. Stepping scrolls the match into view, the selection stays drawn while you use the bar, and a search in a selection shows the stretch it is searching. It is the current document only, and code blocks are not searched.
Changed: The presenter window fits its window better. The slide being shown is now the largest thing in it and the next section is a small picture, on a phone, a laptop or a wide screen. A section with no notes gives their room to the slide.
Added: In the presenter window, the slide being shown can be used. Pictures open full screen with zoom and pan (Escape closes), links open in a new tab, and a long section scrolls, so you can open a picture while sharing the presenter window over a call. The next slide stays a picture.
Changed: The colour scheme control is now one button that goes round System, Light and Dark, with an icon for the one it is on, on the sign-in pages; in the account menu it is one Appearance row that does the same and keeps the menu open. Both say the new scheme to a screen reader.
Changed: The account menu is tidier: your initials, name and email at the top, then Preferences and Appearance, then (for administrators) Organisation settings and Units and workspaces, then Sign out, each with an icon. All the organisation's settings screens are one item, Organisation settings, which has its own navigation.

Twelve collections are public sites and the rest are private; documents have histories by several authors over several months, some have a draft ahead of what is published, and some were never published. Fixed: A document that has never been published no longer says "You have unpublished changes" and that readers still see the published page: there is no published page. Its empty state, which says what is waiting, now also offers a preview of the draft as readers will see it.
Fixed: Opening or folding an accordion item or a details section in the presenter window now does the same in the audience window, and the other way round, so the room sees what the presenter opens. A presenter window opened or reloaded later starts with the same sections open, and a slide you come back to keeps them as you left them.
Changed: Presenting a section taller than the window now scrolls like one long page.
