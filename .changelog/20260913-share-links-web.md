Added: A document can now be shared with somebody who has no account. "Share" in the document header — offered to whoever may manage the document — lists every link on it with what it opens, when it expires, who created it, and when it was last opened; creates one scoped to the document or to the document and everything under it, expiring in 7 days, in 30 days, on a chosen date, or never; shows the address once, with a copy control and the reason it is only shown once; and revokes one behind a confirmation. An organisation that does not allow share links is told so in place, instead of being offered a control that fails.
Added: `/share/<token>` is the page the person you sent a link to opens: the site's name, the document on the same reading surface the application uses, its contents, and one line saying what they hold and when it runs out. A subtree link also lists the documents below its target and reads each one at its own address. It takes no session and sends no cookie, it is `noindex`, and every refusal — an unknown token, an expired or revoked link, a document outside the link's scope — is the same answer, because telling them apart is what a 256-bit token's unguessability rests on not existing.
