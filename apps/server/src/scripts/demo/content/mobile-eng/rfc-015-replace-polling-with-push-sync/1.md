# RFC-015: Replace polling with push sync

**Status:** Proposed, 21 September 2026. **Author:** Jonas Weber. **Reviewers:** Marcus Lee, Aisha Khan (security), platform on-call.

## Context

Skiff learns about changes made elsewhere (a transcript finishing, a teammate editing a note, a theme being assigned) by polling. While the app is in the foreground it calls `GET /v2/sync/pull` every 60 seconds, as described in [Offline sync design](doc:Offline sync design). In the 2026.9 release data, 91% of those calls returned no changes. They are also the largest single part of sync's battery use, 1.3% of a typical day, and they account for 38% of requests to the sync endpoints.

In the background we depend on the operating system's schedule, which gives us a pull every 15 minutes at best and often much less on iOS. A transcript that finishes at 14:02 can appear on a locked phone at 14:40.

## Proposal

Tidepool sends a silent push message to a user's devices whenever the sync log for their workspace advances: APNs background notifications on iOS and high-priority FCM data messages on Android. The payload carries no content, only the workspace id and the new log position. On receipt the app runs a pull. The foreground timer drops from 60 seconds to 5 minutes and stays as a safety net, because push delivery is not guaranteed.

## Options compared

:::table[Ways to learn about changes]{width=wide caption=bottom}
| Option | Typical latency | Battery | Server cost | Works when app is suspended |
| :----- | --------------: | :------ | :---------- | :-------------------------- |
| Poll every 60 s (today) | 30 s | Highest | High: 38% of sync requests | No |
| Silent push, 5 min fallback poll | 2 to 5 s | Lowest | Low: one message per change | Yes, throttled by iOS |
| Server-sent events while foreground | 1 s | Medium | Medium: one open connection per device | No |
| WebSocket | 1 s | Medium | Highest: stateful gateway | No |
:::

Silent push is the only option that helps when the app is not in front of the user. Server-sent events would improve the foreground case further, but they do not work in the background and would need a new gateway.

## Open questions

1. **How reliable is iOS delivery?** Apple throttles silent notifications and may drop them under low power mode. We need a measured delivery rate before we remove the 60-second timer.
2. **Does the push provider need to see anything?** The payload has no note content, but it does name a workspace. Aisha has asked whether a hashed id is enough.
3. **How do we avoid storms?** A busy workspace may advance its log dozens of times a minute. Messages must be collapsed per device.
4. **What do managed devices do?** Harbourview Health's device policy may block background notifications. We should confirm with their IT team.
