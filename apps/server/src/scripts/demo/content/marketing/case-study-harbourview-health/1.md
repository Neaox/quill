# Case study: Harbourview Health

Harbourview Health runs six hospitals and 40 clinics across the coast, with about 11,000 staff. Its patient experience team collects feedback from every one of them, and until this spring most of it sat in spreadsheets for a week or more before anyone read it.

## The problem

Feedback reached the patient experience team from four directions: complaint emails, ward comment cards typed up by hand, a patient survey and notes from nurse managers. About 1,300 items arrived each week. Two analysts spent roughly 15 hours a week reading, tagging and merging duplicates, and a theme usually took nine days to reach the people who could act on it.

> "By the time we noticed that discharge delays were the number one complaint, we had already had three weeks of them. We were reading everything and seeing nothing."
>
> Dr. Helen Osei, Director of Patient Experience, Harbourview Health

## What they did

Harbourview Health started on Tidepool in March 2026. The team imported two years of survey comments from CSV, connected the complaint inbox, and put the in-app widget on the patient portal. Tidepool grouped the feedback into themes automatically, and the analysts spent their time reviewing the groups instead of building them.

## The results after eight weeks

:::table[Before and after]{width=70%}
| Measure | Before | After 8 weeks |
| :------ | -----: | ------------: |
| Analyst hours on triage, per week | 15 | 8 |
| Days from feedback to a named theme | 9 | 3 |
| Items reviewed per week | 1,300 | 1,300 |
| Themes shared with ward leads each month | 2 | 9 |
:::

Triage time fell by 47%. The ward leads now get a short list of themes every Friday, and the discharge delay theme led to a change in how porters are scheduled on two sites.

> "Nobody has asked us to read less feedback. They have asked us to act on more of it, and for the first time we can."
>
> Tomasz Kowalski, Patient Experience Analyst, Harbourview Health

## What comes next

The team is looking at capturing feedback on the wards themselves, from nurse managers on their phones, and is joining the Skiff beta. The goal is to get a ward round's observations into Tidepool on the same day instead of the next Monday.
