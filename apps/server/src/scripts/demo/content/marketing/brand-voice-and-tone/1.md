# Brand voice and tone

We write for product managers, support leads and researchers who are tired of being sold to. Our voice is the one they would use with a sharp colleague: plain, specific and a little dry. This guide covers how we sound wherever Tidepool and Skiff appear, from the website to a push notification.

## Four voice traits

- **Plain.** Short words, short sentences. If a sentence needs a second read, split it.
- **Specific.** A number, a customer name or a screen beats an adjective. "Triage time down 60%" beats "dramatically faster".
- **Warm, not bubbly.** We are glad to help and we do not shout about it. One exclamation mark per piece at most, and never in a headline.
- **Honest.** We say what a feature does not do yet, and we point to the public roadmap instead of hinting.

## Tone changes with the moment

The voice stays constant; the tone moves with what the reader is going through.

| Moment | Tone | Example |
| :----- | :--- | :------ |
| Launch announcement | Confident and brief | "Skiff now keeps your voice notes when you are offline." |
| Error message | Calm and practical | "We couldn't sync 3 notes. They are safe on your phone; we'll retry." |
| Outage or incident | Direct and accountable | "Webhooks were delayed for 2 hours. Here is what happened." |
| Sales email | Respectful of their time | "Two lines on why I'm writing, then a link if it's useful." |

## Do and don't

:::table[Word choices]{width=wide}
| Do | Don't |
| :-- | :---- |
| Write "feedback" | Write "voice of the customer data" |
| Write "themes" in customer-facing copy | Write "clusters", which is an engineering word |
| Write "Tidepool" and "Skiff", capitalised, no "the" | Write "the Tidepool platform" or add ™ |
| Name the customer and the number | Write "leading organisations see big gains" |
| Say "you" and "we" | Say "users" when talking to the person reading |
| Say what changed and why it matters | Open with "We're thrilled to announce" |
:::

## Naming and capitalisation

Product names are always capitalised. Feature names are lower case unless they start a sentence: "the public roadmap", "the feedback widget". Use "iOS and Android", never "mobile platforms". Dates are written "4 November 2026" in long form and "4 Nov" in tables.

## Before you publish

1. Put the piece on the [editorial calendar](doc:Editorial calendar Q4) before you write it, with an owner and a date.
2. Read it aloud once. Anything you stumble on gets rewritten.
3. Check every number against its source and put the source in the draft comments.
4. Use the boilerplate and product descriptions in the [press kit](doc:Press kit) rather than writing new ones.
