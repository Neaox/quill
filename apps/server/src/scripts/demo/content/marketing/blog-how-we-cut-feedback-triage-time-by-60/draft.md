Every Friday morning, our patient experience team used to open a spreadsheet with about 1,300 rows and start reading. Six hospitals, 40 clinics, four different sources of feedback, and two analysts to make sense of it all. It took roughly 15 hours a week, and by the time a pattern was visible it was nine days old.

Since March we have done it differently. Triage now takes about 6 hours a week, and a theme reaches a ward lead in two days. This is what we changed, and what we would do again.

## Stop tagging by hand

Our old process depended on tags that two people applied slightly differently. "Discharge delay", "waiting to go home" and "slow paperwork" were three tags for one problem. We imported two years of survey comments into Tidepool and let it group them into themes. Then we did the part that matters: we renamed the themes in our own words and merged the ones that were really the same.

## Put the inbox and the portal in one place

We connected the complaints inbox and added the feedback widget to the patient portal. Nothing new was asked of patients; their comments just stopped living in four places.

The ward leads get nine to fourteen themes a month, each with a handful of real comments. Nobody reads a report. They read the top three and decide.

## What changed on the wards

The discharge delay theme was the first one that moved something. Porters were being scheduled by site, not by discharge time, so on two sites patients waited for transport after they were medically ready. After a change to the rota, complaints about discharge waits fell by a third between April and September.

## What we got wrong

We spent the first three weeks trying to keep our old tags as a second layer. It slowed us down and nobody used it. We switched it off in week four.

## What is next

Our nurse managers are testing the Skiff 2.0 beta on two hospitals. They record a voice note at the end of a round and see the transcript as they speak. We will report back once we have a full month of data.

If you want to try the same approach with your own team, start with a CSV of last quarter's comments. It took us an afternoon, and the first themes appeared before we had finished our coffee.
