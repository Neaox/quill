# Design principles

Tideline is the design system behind Tidepool and Skiff. These principles are not slogans for a wall. They are what we reach for when two reasonable people disagree about a pull request, a component API or a screen, and need a tie-breaker that does not depend on who is more senior.

:::cards{variant=ruled}
### Clarity before cleverness

A person scanning a feedback inbox between meetings should never have to decode an interface. Use the plain word ("Archive", not "Park"), the standard control and the conventional position. Novel interaction is allowed only where it saves at least one step for most people, and it must still work with a keyboard alone.

### Dense, never cramped

Product teams review hundreds of items a day, so we favour information density: 14px body text in tables, 32px rows by default. Density comes from tight, consistent rhythm on the 4px grid, not from shrinking type below 12px or squeezing touch targets under 44px on Skiff.

### Accessible by default

Contrast, focus rings, labels and keyboard order are built into the component, so that using it correctly is the easy path. A component that needs the consumer to remember an ARIA attribute is a bug in the component. The [Accessibility checklist](doc:Accessibility checklist) is the bar for every release.

### Show the state of things

Every screen answers three questions without being asked: what is happening, what just happened, and what can I do next. Saving, syncing, offline and failed are visible states with their own wording and treatment, never a spinner that quietly gives up. On Skiff, a note captured offline shows "Waiting to sync" until the server confirms it.

### One system, two surfaces

Tidepool on the web and Skiff on iOS and Android share tokens, names and behaviour. A theme is changed in one place and arrives in both. Platform conventions win where they clash with ours: a back swipe on iOS is not up for debate.
:::

## How we use them

Principles come up in three places: design critiques on Thursdays, component proposals in the Tideline repository, and code review. In a proposal, name the principle you are serving and the one you are trading against. "This adds a third button variant, which costs us clarity, because the confirm step in bulk archive kept being missed" is a useful sentence. "It looks nicer" is not.

## When principles collide

They will. The order we use, from strongest to weakest:

1. Accessible by default. This one is never traded away.
2. Clarity before cleverness.
3. Show the state of things.
4. One system, two surfaces.
5. Dense, never cramped.

If a denser layout fails the 24px minimum target size in WCAG 2.5.8, density loses. If a platform convention breaks cross-surface consistency, the platform wins and we document the exception in the component page.

## Requesting an exception

Teams ship against deadlines, and sometimes a screen cannot meet every principle on the day. Open an issue in the Tideline repository with the screen, the principle it bends and the date you will fix it. Sofia Alvarez or a design system maintainer replies within two working days. Exceptions against the first principle are not granted; we help you find another way.
