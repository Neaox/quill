# Capacity planning

The platform team reviews capacity on the first Monday of each month. The rule is simple: every tier keeps its peak below 60% of what is provisioned, and we start a change when a forecast says we will cross 70% within a quarter. The 60% leaves room for losing an availability zone, which takes a third of our nodes with it. Topology is described in [Platform overview](doc:Platform overview).

That is about $6.30 a month for each of the 3,520 workspaces that are active in a given week, and about 4% of revenue. Finance wants infrastructure to stay under 6%.

## How fast we are growing

:::table[Demand, nine months apart]{caption=bottom}

| :------ | -----------: | -----------: | -----: |

| Feedback items stored (millions) | 61 | 151 | +148% |
| PostgreSQL size (TB) | 1.1 | 2.1 | +91% |
| Webhook deliveries per day (millions) | 3.2 | 8.4 | +163% |
| Voice notes per day (thousands) | 0 | 22 | n/a |

Two things drive nearly all of it. Harbourview Health and Northwind Retail both moved their support ticket sync onto Tidepool in spring, which more than doubled webhook volume; and Skiff voice notes went live in May. Since August webhooks run on their own queue and eight pods, and transcription has its own pool, which is where most of the six new nodes went. Storage grows by about 55 GB a month.

## Headroom today

1. **PostgreSQL CPU.** The p95 over the last 30 days is 41% on the primary, so there is room.
2. **PostgreSQL connections.** 470 of 800 used at peak, through PgBouncer. Do not raise `max_connections`; add pooler capacity.
3. **Redis memory.** 38% of memory used. BullMQ keeps completed jobs for 24 hours, which is most of it.
4. **Nodes.** Peak CPU requests are 54% of allocatable. Losing a zone would push that to 81%, which we accept for the length of an incident.
5. **Disk.** Autoscaling storage is enabled with a ceiling of 4 TB; an alarm at 3 TB asks a person to look.

## Decisions

- We will not move to a bigger primary until the p95 reaches 60% for two weeks running. The January forecast stops short of it, but the Skiff 2.0 launch could close the gap, so Marcus Lee will re-run the numbers on 30 November. The next size up, `db.r6g.8xlarge`, doubles the database bill to about $9,900.
- The `eu-west-1` standby stays at 12 nodes. Scaling it up is part of the failover and rehearsed every quarter.
- Transcription nodes move to a separate Karpenter pool of Spot instances, saving about $900 a month.
- Reserved-instance coverage is reviewed every January and July.
