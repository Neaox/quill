# Capacity planning

The platform team reviews capacity on the first Monday of each month. The rule is simple: every tier keeps its peak below 60% of what is provisioned, and we start a change when a forecast says we will cross 70% within a quarter. The 60% leaves room for losing an availability zone, which takes a third of our nodes with it. Topology is described in [Platform overview](doc:Platform overview).

## What we run and what it costs

Figures are from the July review. Prices are on-demand rates after our reserved-instance discount of roughly 35% on nodes and databases.

That is about $6 a month for each of the 3,300 workspaces that are active in a given week, and about 4% of revenue. Finance wants infrastructure to stay under 6%.

Two things drive nearly all of it. Harbourview Health and Northwind Retail both moved their support ticket sync onto Tidepool in spring, which more than doubled webhook volume; and Skiff voice notes went live in May. Storage grows by about 65 GB a month.

## Headroom today

1. **PostgreSQL CPU.** The p95 over the last 30 days is 38% on the primary, so there is room.
2. **PostgreSQL connections.** 410 of 800 used at peak, through PgBouncer. Do not raise `max_connections`; add pooler capacity.
3. **Redis memory.** 34% of memory used. BullMQ keeps completed jobs for 24 hours, which is most of it.
4. **Nodes.** Peak CPU requests are 52% of allocatable. Losing a zone would push that to 78%, which we accept for the length of an incident.
5. **Disk.** Autoscaling storage is enabled with a ceiling of 4 TB; an alarm at 3 TB asks a person to look.

## Decisions

- We will not move to a bigger primary until the p95 reaches 60% for two weeks running. The next size up, `db.r6g.8xlarge`, doubles the database bill to about $9,900.
- The `eu-west-1` standby stays at 12 nodes. Scaling it up is part of the failover and rehearsed every quarter.
- Reserved-instance coverage is reviewed every January and July.
