# Postmortem: 14 August webhook delays

:small[Severity SEV2. Written by Marcus Lee with the on-call engineer and Tom Becker. Blameless format; see the [Incident response process](doc:Incident response process).]

## Summary

On Friday 14 August 2026, between 09:12 and 10:46 UTC, webhook deliveries to customers were delayed by up to 94 minutes. About 520,000 deliveries to 412 workspaces were late. None were lost. The cause was a 410,000-row CSV import from Northwind Retail that filled the shared `default` queue and left webhook jobs waiting behind it. We found out from Lantern Logistics, not from an alert.

## Impact

- **Webhooks.** 520,000 deliveries, to 412 of roughly 3,300 active workspaces, arrived between 5 and 94 minutes late. Workspaces that use webhooks to sync tickets and warehouse events were hit hardest.
- **Lantern Logistics.** Their dispatch board is driven by our `feedback.created` webhooks and showed no new customer messages for an hour. They raised a ticket at 09:58 and a call with their account owner followed.
- **Northwind Retail.** Their import ran for 3 hours and 20 minutes instead of the usual 40 minutes for a file that size, because we throttled it once we found it.
- **Everyone else.** Imports and cluster refreshes from other workspaces were slower than normal for about 90 minutes. Nothing failed.

## Root cause

All background work in Tidepool shared a single BullMQ queue, `default`, served by one pool of workers. BullMQ processes jobs in the order they were added, so a single customer enqueueing 410,000 jobs can put every later job, from every customer, behind it. Nothing limited how many jobs one workspace could have in flight.

Two other things made it worse:

- Webhook retries were re-added to the same queue, so each failed delivery went to the back of the line again.
- Our only queue alert measured depth rather than age, and it was set to ticket severity because a deep queue is normal during large imports.

:::details[Why did the import create one job per row?]
It was a simple design from 2024, when imports were capped at 5,000 rows. The cap rose to 500,000 rows in March for enterprise plans, and the job model was not revisited. A batch of 500 rows per job would have used 820 jobs here, not 410,000.
:::

:::details[Why didn't scaling the workers help?]
More workers drain the queue faster, but the webhook jobs were still at the back of 380,000 others. Doubling the workers halved the wait; it did not remove it. Only taking webhooks out of the queue did that.
:::

## What went well

- Support declared the incident four minutes after the ticket without waiting for an engineer.
- Nothing was lost. Pausing the import was a flag, not a deploy.
- Webhook delivery is at-least-once with idempotency keys, so recovery needed no replay and no customer saw duplicates.

## What went badly

- A customer found it before we did, 46 minutes before any alert.
- We took 16 minutes from the report to the cause, partly because no dashboard showed the age of the oldest job in each queue.
- The first fix, adding workers, did not address the problem.
