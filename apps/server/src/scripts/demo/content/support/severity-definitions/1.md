# Severity definitions

Set the severity when you open or triage a ticket, and change it when the facts change. The levels match the ones engineering uses in incidents, so a Sev 1 means the same thing to everyone.

- **Sev 1: outage or data risk.** Tidepool is down or unusable for many customers, data is lost or exposed, or no one can sign in to Skiff. First response within 15 minutes, 24 hours a day, for Business and Enterprise.
- **Sev 2: major feature broken.** A core function such as importing, clustering, publishing a roadmap or syncing Skiff is failing for one Business or Enterprise customer, or for many customers with a workaround. First response within 1 hour.
- **Sev 3: degraded.** Something works badly or only for some users, and the customer has a workaround. First response within 4 working hours on Business, 1 working day on Team and 2 on Free.
- **Sev 4: question or request.** How-to questions, feature requests, cosmetic bugs. First response within 3 working days.

Severity follows the impact, not the tone of the message. A polite report of leaked data is Sev 1; a furious one about a typo is Sev 4. Customers on Enterprise plans keep the response times in their contract, which may be shorter.

When in doubt go one level higher and say why in a private note. To find out who to call, use the [Escalation matrix](doc:Escalation matrix).
