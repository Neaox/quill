# Handling an angry customer

Most angry customers are not angry at you. They are stuck, they have lost time, and someone before you has usually told them it would be fine. Your job in the first two minutes is to make them feel heard and then to make one promise you can keep.

## The first reply

1. Read the whole ticket, including earlier replies, before you type. Nothing angers a customer faster than being asked something they already answered.
2. Name the problem in their words and say what it cost them. "Your Zendesk sync has been stalled since Tuesday, and your team has been sorting feedback by hand."
3. Say what happens next and when. Give a time, not "soon". "I'm checking the sync logs now and will write back by 15:00 your time, even if I don't have the fix yet."
4. Sign with your own name. Customers at Pinecrest Bank and Northwind Retail tell us they remember the people who answered, not the queue.

> [!TIP]
> Skip "I understand your frustration". It reads as a script. State the specific thing that went wrong instead; it proves you read the ticket.

## What not to say

- "As per our documentation..." Even when it is true, it sounds like blame.
- "That is not supported." Say what is supported and what you can do today.
- "Unfortunately" more than once in a message.
- Any date for engineering work. Say what you know and when you will next update them.

## When to bring in a lead

Ask Tom Becker or the lead on shift to join the ticket when any of these is true:

- The customer has written in three times in 48 hours about the same problem.
- They are on Business or Enterprise and the problem is blocking their team. Check the [escalation matrix](doc:Escalation matrix) for the severity and the right owner.
- They mention cancelling, a chargeback, a regulator or a lawyer.
- You have been working the ticket for more than 90 minutes without progress.

Do this early. A lead who joins at the first sign of trouble can usually save a ticket; a lead who joins at the cancellation threat usually can't.

> [!WARNING]
> Never agree to a refund, credit or contract change on your own because the customer is angry. Offer what the [refund and credit policy](doc:Refund and credit policy) allows at your level and bring in a lead for anything beyond it.

## After it is resolved

Within one working day, write a short follow-up: what went wrong, what we did, and what changes so it does not happen again. If the cause was a product bug, link the ticket to the engineering issue so the customer can be told when it ships. Add a private note on the account with what worked, so the next agent starts warm.

Take a few minutes away from the queue after a hard conversation. Tell your lead if you need to hand off the next one.
