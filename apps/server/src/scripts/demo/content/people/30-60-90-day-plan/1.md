# 30-60-90 day plan

A starting point for a new engineer's first three months at Acme. The hiring manager copies it into the new hire's onboarding page on day one and edits it with them; the goals below are defaults, not a contract. Nobody is expected to ship to production in week one, but everyone is expected to have merged something small by the end of week two.

## Before day one

- [ ] Laptop ordered and shipped to arrive two working days before the start date
- [ ] Accounts created: Google Workspace, Slack, GitHub, Linear
- [ ] A buddy assigned, from a different team where possible
- [ ] First week's calendar sent: team intro, 1:1 with the manager, platform overview talk

## First 30 days: learn

The goal is to understand how a piece of feedback travels through Tidepool and to make a first small change.

- [ ] Set up the development environment and run the full test suite locally
- [ ] Read the platform overview and the architecture notes for your own area
- [ ] Merge a first pull request in week two, a bug fix or a small improvement picked from the starter list
- [ ] Shadow one on-call shift without being on the rota
- [ ] Meet every member of your team one to one, and two people in support
- [ ] Agree a written plan for days 31 to 90 with your manager

## Days 31 to 60: contribute

- [ ] Own a small feature from ticket to release, with a short design note for review
- [ ] Review at least five pull requests from teammates
- [ ] Join the on-call rota as secondary
- [ ] Spend a morning with Tom Becker's support team, reading real tickets about your area
- [ ] First career conversation with your manager: strengths, gaps, what you want from the year

## Days 61 to 90: own

- [ ] Lead a piece of work that spans more than one sprint
- [ ] Write or improve one page of documentation that you wished you had in week one
- [ ] Give a ten-minute demo at the team's sprint review
- [ ] Complete a 90-day check-in using the questions below

## The 90-day check-in

Your manager asks, and writes down the answers:

1. What has surprised you, good or bad?
2. What has been harder to find out than it should have been?
3. Which of your first three months' goals felt wrong, and why?
4. What should we change for the next person who joins?

> [!TIP]
> If the answer to the second question is "everything", say so in week three, not week twelve. Fixing onboarding is part of the job for the whole team.
