# Onboarding friction: interview synthesis

Between 3 and 28 August, Sofia's researcher and I interviewed nine people who created a Tidepool workspace in the previous 30 days. Each session was 45 minutes, recorded with consent, and started with "show me what you did on your first day." This is what we heard, grouped by where people got stuck. Interview notes for the two longest sessions are in [Interview notes: Harbourview Health](doc:Interview notes: Harbourview Health) and [Interview notes: Northwind Retail](doc:Interview notes: Northwind Retail).

## Who we talked to

Five product managers, two researchers, one support lead and one founder. Four were on the Starter plan, three on Team, two on Business. Six came from software companies, two from healthcare and one from retail. We deliberately included three people whose workspace had no feedback in it after a week, because they are the ones our usage dashboards cannot explain.

## What we found

:::table[Friction points, nine interviews]{width=wide caption=bottom}
| Friction point | People affected | Median time lost | Severity |
| :------------- | --------------: | ---------------: | :------- |
| Mapping CSV columns on import | 6 | 35 min | High |
| Not knowing what a theme is | 5 | n/a | High |
| Waiting for a developer to install the widget | 4 | 3 days | High |
| Inviting the team before there is content | 4 | 10 min | Medium |
| Choosing a plan while evaluating | 3 | 15 min | Low |
:::

### The empty workspace teaches nothing

Five of nine could not say, after their first session, what Tidepool does with feedback once it is in. The product asked them to import first and explained themes later.

> "I put in 400 rows and it said 'processing'. Then I had a list. I thought, so it's a spreadsheet?"
> Product manager, Starter plan

> "Nobody told me the themes would write themselves. I was about to start making them by hand."
> Researcher, Business plan

### Importing is where people give up

Six people hit a column-mapping step with no preview. Two abandoned it, and both had no feedback in their workspace a week later.

> "It wanted me to say which column was the 'source'. I don't have a source column. I have a column called Where."
> Support lead, Team plan

### Dependence on a developer

Four wanted the in-app widget and none could install it themselves. The median wait was three days. One founder pasted the snippet into the wrong page template and did not notice for a week.

## What differed by segment

::::accordion{name=segments}
:::details[Starter plan: they are evaluating, not adopting]
Starter users spend their first session deciding whether Tidepool is worth a conversation with their manager. They want a result in 20 minutes. Importing a sample file and seeing themes appear is what converts them; those who got there stayed, and those who did not rarely came back.
:::
:::details[Team plan: the widget and the integration decide it]
Team customers already have a support desk and a website. Their first-week success depends on connecting at least one live source. Waiting on a developer was the main reason two of them had fewer than ten items after a fortnight.
:::
:::details[Business plan: security questions come first]
Both Business interviewees asked about data location and single sign-on before touching the product. One had been told by procurement that nothing could be imported until the security review was complete, which took five weeks.
:::
::::

## What people said they wanted

- A sample workspace to look at before importing anything.
- A preview of the first ten rows during CSV import, with a guess at each column.
- A "send this to my developer" link for the widget snippet.

:::details[How we ran the interviews]
Sessions were remote, 45 minutes. We asked people to share their screen and replay their first day. We did not show our own product until the last ten minutes. Notes were taken live, tagged by two people independently, and reconciled afterwards; we disagreed on 11 of 94 tags.
:::
