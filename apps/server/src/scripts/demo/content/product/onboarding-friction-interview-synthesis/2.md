# Onboarding friction: interview synthesis

Between 3 August and 25 September, Sofia's researcher and I interviewed fourteen people who created a Tidepool workspace in the previous 30 days. Each session was 45 minutes, recorded with consent, and started with "show me what you did on your first day." This is what we heard, grouped by where people got stuck. Interview notes for the two longest sessions are in [Interview notes: Harbourview Health](doc:Interview notes: Harbourview Health) and [Interview notes: Northwind Retail](doc:Interview notes: Northwind Retail).

## Who we talked to

Eight product managers, three researchers, two support leads and one founder. Six were on the Starter plan, five on Team, three on Business. Nine came from software companies, three from healthcare and two from retail. We deliberately included four people whose workspace had no feedback in it after a week, because they are the ones our usage dashboards cannot explain.

## What we found

:::table[Friction points, fourteen interviews]{width=wide caption=bottom}
| Friction point | People affected | Median time lost | Severity |
| :------------- | --------------: | ---------------: | :------- |
| Mapping CSV columns on import | 9 | 35 min | High |
| Not knowing what a theme is | 8 | n/a | High |
| Waiting for a developer to install the widget | 6 | 3 days | High |
| Inviting the team before there is content | 5 | 10 min | Medium |
| Choosing a plan while evaluating | 4 | 15 min | Low |
:::

### The empty workspace teaches nothing

Eight of fourteen could not say, after their first session, what Tidepool does with feedback once it is in. The product asked them to import first and explained themes later.

> "I put in 400 rows and it said 'processing'. Then I had a list. I thought, so it's a spreadsheet?"
> Product manager, Starter plan

> "Nobody told me the themes would write themselves. I was about to start making them by hand."
> Researcher, Business plan

### Importing is where people give up

Nine people hit a column-mapping step with no preview. Three abandoned it, and all three had no feedback in their workspace a week later.

> "It wanted me to say which column was the 'source'. I don't have a source column. I have a column called Where."
> Support lead, Team plan

### Dependence on a developer

Six wanted the in-app widget and none could install it themselves. The median wait was three days. One founder pasted the snippet into the wrong page template and did not notice for a week.

### The team arrives before the content

Five people invited colleagues on day one, and those colleagues found an empty workspace. Three said they would not invite again until they had something to show.

> "I invited my whole squad and then felt embarrassed. There was nothing in there."
> Product manager, Team plan

## What differed by segment

::::accordion{name=segments}
:::details[Starter plan: they are evaluating, not adopting]
Starter users spend their first session deciding whether Tidepool is worth a conversation with their manager. They want a result in 20 minutes. Importing a sample file and seeing themes appear is what converts them; those who got there stayed, and those who did not rarely came back.
:::
:::details[Team plan: the widget and the integration decide it]
Team customers already have a support desk and a website. Their first-week success depends on connecting at least one live source. Waiting on a developer was the main reason three of them had fewer than ten items after a fortnight.
:::
:::details[Business plan: security questions come first]
All three Business interviewees asked about data location and single sign-on before touching the product. One had been told by procurement that nothing could be imported until the security review was complete, which took five weeks.
:::
::::

## What people said they wanted

- A sample workspace to look at before importing anything.
- A preview of the first ten rows during CSV import, with a guess at each column.
- A "send this to my developer" link for the widget snippet.
- Invitations that can wait until the workspace has its first theme.

## What we recommend

1. **Seed a sample workspace** with 60 realistic items and four themes on sign-up, and remove it with one click. Sofia's team estimates three weeks of design and two of engineering.
2. **Preview the import.** Show the first ten rows, guess columns from their headers, and let people proceed with only a text column mapped. This is the cheapest fix and addresses the most severe, most common problem.
3. **Make the widget shareable.** A page with the snippet, copy button and a test checklist that a developer can open without a login.
4. **Delay the invite prompt** until the first theme exists.

:::callout[What we are not recommending]{type=note}
We considered a guided product tour. Of fourteen people, only two said they would use one, and both were the ones who already understood themes. The sample workspace does the same job without asking anyone to click through a tooltip.
:::

The first two items are candidates for Q1; see [Q1 2027 themes and bets](doc:Q1 2027 themes and bets) for what fits.
:::details[How we ran the interviews]
Sessions were remote, 45 minutes. We asked people to share their screen and replay their first day. We did not show our own product until the last ten minutes. Notes were taken live, tagged by two people independently, and reconciled afterwards; we disagreed on 17 of 161 tags.
:::

## What we do not know yet

- **Whether the fixes change retention.** Everyone we spoke to had created a workspace and agreed to be interviewed, so we have heard nothing from people who signed up and vanished. Tom's team will contact 30 of them in November.
- **How much of the import problem is our template.** Our sample CSV has a column called `source`; real exports from Zendesk and spreadsheets almost never do. A cleaner sample would hide the problem from our own demos.
- **Whether non-English teams struggle more.** Two of the fourteen worked in German and French. Both found the import step harder, but two is not a pattern.
