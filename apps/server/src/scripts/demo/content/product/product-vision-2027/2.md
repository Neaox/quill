# Product vision 2027

::toc{min=2 max=2}

## Where we are

Tidepool has 2,300 paying workspaces, from five-person startups to Harbourview Health's 300-seat research team. Those workspaces send us about 38 million pieces of feedback a month through four doors: support tickets, interviews, the in-app widget and CSV imports. Skiff, launched in 2025, now has 11,200 weekly active users who capture notes in the field and sync them later.

Collecting feedback is no longer the hard part. The median workspace holds 3,100 items that nobody has read, and in our last round of interviews the median time from a customer asking for something to a team deciding about it was 19 days. Teams drown, then they stop trusting the inbox, then they go back to spreadsheets.

:::notes
Open with the 19-day number. Ask the room how long it takes on their own team before showing the next slide.
:::

## Who we build for

Three kinds of team, in this order of priority.

- **Product teams of 5 to 40 people** at software companies, which are 71% of workspaces. They own the roadmap and read feedback every week. Oakline Studios is typical: four product managers, one researcher and a support lead who forwards the awkward tickets.
- **Research and operations teams in regulated industries**, such as Harbourview Health and Pinecrest Bank. They have fewer workspaces but run the largest ones, and they need audit history and clear data handling before they will adopt anything.
- **Field and retail teams** like Northwind Retail and Lantern Logistics, whose people meet customers away from a desk. This is why Skiff exists.

We do not build for individual consultants or for teams that only want a voting board. Both are well served by cheaper tools, and chasing them pulls the product towards simplicity we cannot charge for.

## What customers told us

In 22 interviews between July and September, three sentences came up again and again:

> "We have the feedback. I just can't tell which of it matters."
> Product lead, Northwind Retail

> "My team records everything in the field and then it sits in a notes app."
> Operations manager, Lantern Logistics

> "If I tell a customer we're building it, I need to be able to show them where it is."
> Head of research, Harbourview Health

Each of the three bets answers one of these.

## The vision

By the end of 2027, a product team using Tidepool can answer "what should we build next, and why?" in an afternoon, and every customer who asked can see what happened to their request.

## Principles

:::cards{variant=ruled}
### Capture everything

Nothing a customer says is lost, whichever door it came through. Skiff and the widget exist so that the quick note on a train platform counts as much as a formal interview.

### Themes before tickets

People decide about problems, not about individual requests. Every piece of feedback should land in a theme within minutes, and a theme should be readable without opening its evidence.

### Decide in public

The roadmap is a promise. Customers see what is planned, what shipped and what we decided against, with the reason.

### Evidence over volume

A theme backed by six hospital customers is not the same as a theme backed by sixty free-plan users. We show who is asking, not only how many.
:::

## Three bets for 2027

1. **Clustering that people trust.** Today's clustering misgroups about one item in five and cannot explain itself. We are rebuilding it around embeddings with visible reasons. See [Spec: Feedback clustering v2](doc:Spec: Feedback clustering v2).
2. **Skiff as the field instrument.** Voice notes become transcripts that go straight into themes, and offline work stops being a second-class path. See [Spec: Skiff voice notes](doc:Spec: Skiff voice notes).
3. **Roadmaps that close the loop.** When a theme ships, every customer who contributed to it hears about it without anyone writing an email.

> [!WARNING]
> Three bets is the limit. If a fourth is proposed, one of these three has to be dropped, in writing, in this document.

## What we will not do

- Build a general project management tool. We hand off to Jira and Linear; we do not replace them.
- Score individual customers' sentiment. Themes carry sentiment; people do not.
- Offer per-customer custom fields before the clustering rebuild ships, because they would harden the old model.
- Chase a self-serve enterprise tier in the first half of the year.

## Open questions

- Do hospitals and banks need a region-pinned data residency option before the end of 2027? Harbourview Health and Pinecrest Bank both raised it.
- Is a voice note a document or a piece of feedback? The answer changes how long we keep audio.
