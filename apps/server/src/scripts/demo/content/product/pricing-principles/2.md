# Pricing principles

These are the rules we hold every pricing proposal against. They exist because the last two repricings (2024 and spring 2026) were argued from competitors' price pages rather than from how customers get value. Ravi, Priya and finance own them jointly.

## The principles

1. **Charge for the people who decide, not the people who contribute.** An editor reads themes, merges them and publishes the roadmap. A contributor sends feedback. Contributors and viewers are always free and unlimited, because every one of them makes the product more valuable to the editors.
2. **Skiff is included.** Nobody pays separately for the mobile app. A field team's notes are the best evidence we get, and a surcharge would reduce it.
3. **Volume is not the meter.** We do not charge per feedback item. A customer who imports 200,000 old tickets should be pleased with us, not surprised by an invoice.
4. **A plan should be explainable in one sentence.** If sales needs a calculator, the plan is wrong.
5. **Annual is a discount, not a trap.** Annual billing is 15% cheaper. Cancelling mid-term gets a pro-rata credit, as described in our refund policy.
6. **New capability goes into the plan that needs it, not into an add-on.** Clustering v2 will ship in Team and above. We considered an "intelligence" add-on at $9 per editor and rejected it: it would make the plan table longer without helping anyone decide.

## Where we are exposed

> [!NOTE]
> About 40% of Team workspaces have exactly ten editors. That is the plan ceiling, not demand: we suspect they are rationing seats. In September we checked: 61% of those workspaces have more than ten people who open themes weekly. The ceiling moves to 15 on 1 January 2027 with no price change.

Business at $39 sits close to two competitors who include unlimited editors. The answer is to keep to principle 1 and make editors clearly worth their price, not to discount. See [Competitive landscape](doc:Competitive landscape) for the comparison.

## Process

Any change to the table above needs a one-page proposal naming the principle it serves, the customers it affects (count them in the billing export, do not estimate) and what we say to the 20 largest accounts. Priya signs off; finance models the revenue effect. The 2027 direction these principles serve is set out in [Product vision 2027](doc:Product vision 2027).
