# Vendor risk assessment

Before any supplier receives Acme or customer data, or gets a connection into our systems, the team that wants it files a request with the security team. This guide explains what we check and how long it takes. It supports SOC 2 control CC9.2 and the supplier rules in the [Information security policy](doc:Information security policy).

## Tier the vendor first

| Tier | What the vendor touches | Examples | Depth of review |
| :--- | :---------------------- | :------- | :-------------- |
| 1 | Restricted data, or runs in our production path | AWS, the speech-to-text provider for Skiff voice notes, our email delivery service | Full, repeated every year |
| 2 | Confidential data, or employee data | Datadog, Zendesk, the HR system | Standard, repeated every two years |
| 3 | Internal or public data only | Design tools, survey tools | Light, at purchase |

When in doubt, choose the higher tier. Aisha Khan makes the final call.

## What you send

- A one-paragraph business case, and who in Acme will own the relationship
- The data the vendor will see, and where it is stored (region matters for customers on EU terms)
- A link to the vendor's security page and, for Tier 1 and 2, their latest SOC 2 report or ISO 27001 certificate

## The assessment

The security team aims to answer within 10 working days for Tier 1 and 5 for Tier 2 and 3. Work through the checklist and record the evidence in the vendor's file.

- [ ] Vendor holds a current SOC 2 Type II report or ISO 27001 certificate, and the report scope covers the service we will use
- [ ] Complementary user entity controls in the report are listed and we meet each one
- [ ] Data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and the vendor states where it lives
- [ ] Single sign-on and role-based access are available, and we will turn them on
- [ ] The data processing agreement is signed, with a breach notice period of 72 hours or less
- [ ] Sub-processors are listed, and we have a way to be told about changes
- [ ] Data is deleted or returned within 30 days of contract end
- [ ] A named security contact exists for incidents
- [ ] The vendor is added to the supplier register with its tier, owner and next review date

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A vendor with no independent audit report is not an automatic no, but it is a Tier 1 conclusion for anything it can read. Compensating controls such as a customer-held encryption key need the security lead's written approval.

## After approval

The supplier register is reviewed each quarter. Tier 1 vendors are reassessed each year, and sooner if they report a breach. Evidence for these reviews is part of our [SOC 2 Type II readiness](doc:SOC 2 Type II readiness).
