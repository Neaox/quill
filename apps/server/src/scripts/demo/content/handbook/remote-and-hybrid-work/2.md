# Remote and hybrid work

We hire for the job, not for the postcode. You can work from home, from one of our offices, or from a mix of the two, as long as your team can rely on you and you can rely on them. This is what that means in practice.

## Where you work

- **Remote:** you work from home and come to the office for planned team gatherings. You can live anywhere in a country where we employ people. Dana Okafor's team keeps the list; ask before you plan a move.
- **Hybrid:** you work from an office on your team's anchor days, which are Tuesday and Thursday by default, and from wherever suits you on the other days.
- **Office:** you are welcome in any office, any day. Desks are first come, first served, apart from the dedicated ones for people who sit in the office four days or more.

You choose when you join and can change later, with your manager's agreement and a month's notice if it affects a desk or your team's anchor days.

## Working hours

We care about overlap, not hours on a clock. Every team agrees core hours of at least four hours that suit everyone, such as 10:00 to 14:00 in the timezone most of the team is in. Be reachable then. Outside them, work when you work best, and do not expect an answer from a colleague late in their evening. [Meeting norms](doc:Meeting norms) explain how we schedule across timezones.

Teams spread across more than four timezones, including the platform team, write things down so work can continue while someone sleeps.

## Equipment and home office

New joiners receive a laptop, a monitor and a headset before day one. For your home office, you can claim a one-off $750 for a desk, chair or lighting, and $40 a month towards internet. Claim through the process in [Expenses and travel](doc:Expenses and travel). You keep the equipment if you leave after a year; before that, return the laptop and monitor only.

## Coming together

Each team meets in person at least once a quarter, and the whole company once a year. We pay for travel and a hotel, and we plan the dates at least eight weeks ahead so you can arrange care and leave.

## Working from another country

You may work from another country for up to 20 working days a year, as long as it is within the EU, UK, US or Canada and you have your manager's agreement. Put it in the calendar and tell people operations at least 4 weeks ahead, so we can check tax and insurance. Anything longer, or anywhere else, needs approval from people operations first, because of tax, immigration and insurance rules.

Security allows no work on company laptops in countries on the restricted list, which Aisha Khan's team publishes. You are on call? Then you need to be somewhere with a reliable connection and you must tell the rota owner before you travel.
