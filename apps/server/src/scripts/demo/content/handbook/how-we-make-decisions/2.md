# How we make decisions

With 240 people we cannot decide everything together, and we do not want one person deciding everything. So we try to put each decision with the person closest to it, and to make the thinking visible so anyone can challenge it.

## One name per decision

Every decision has one **directly responsible individual** (DRI). That person gathers input, makes the call, and writes it down. Committees advise; they do not decide. If you are not sure who the DRI is, ask your manager. If nobody knows, that is the first thing to fix.

## Reversible or not

Before you decide, ask whether you could undo it in a week.

- **Reversible** (a copy change, an internal tool, a meeting time): decide today, tell the people affected, and move on. Do not wait for consensus.
- **Hard to reverse** (pricing, a new database, a hire, a customer commitment, anything touching security or data retention): write a short proposal and leave it open for comment before you decide.

> [!NOTE]
> Most decisions are reversible. If you find yourself asking for sign-off on a reversible one, you are probably slowing us down.

## Writing a proposal

Use an RFC for engineering changes and a one-page proposal in Quill for everything else. It should say:

1. The problem, in two or three sentences, and who has it.
2. The options you considered, including doing nothing.
3. What you recommend and what it costs: time, money, risk.
4. Who is affected and who must be consulted.
5. The date you will decide.

Post it in the relevant channel and tag the people it affects. Give it at least 5 working days for comments, so people in every timezone and anyone on leave can reach it. Anyone can comment; only the DRI decides. If you disagree, say so with a reason and a better option.

## After the decision

The DRI records the outcome in the proposal: what was decided, why, and what would make us revisit it. We keep these as decision records, for example the ADRs and RFCs in engineering, so the next person does not have to guess. Then tell the people who commented what you chose, whether or not it went their way.

## When we disagree

Say what you think, once, with evidence. If the DRI decides differently, support it as if it were your own. If it turns out to be wrong, the person who raised the concern is the first to say so, and nobody says "I told you so". This follows from [Our values](doc:Our values): say the hard thing early, then own the outcome together.

### Disagree and commit

Sometimes the call has to be made in a meeting, such as [a short one](doc:Meeting norms) before a release. The DRI asks "can everyone commit?" and records any objections by name in the notes. Commit means you will not quietly work against it. It does not mean you agree.

Decisions about pay, hiring and performance are different: they stay confidential to the people involved, and people operations owns the process.
